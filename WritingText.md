FHN Insurance Legacy Application to .NET — Development Estimation and 4-Month Delivery Plan

Objective

Analyze the existing FHN Insurance legacy application and the provided estimation PowerPoint. Prepare a professional, leadership-ready PowerPoint presentation with a maximum of 3 slides that justifies a realistic development-only timeline of up to 4 months for one developer to rebuild/modernize the application in .NET.

The presentation will be shared with business stakeholders and leadership. It must be based on the actual application, defensible calculations, and clearly documented assumptions. Do not invent figures or make unsupported delivery commitments.

1. Analyze the Existing Legacy Application

First, inspect the complete available legacy application, source code, screens, workflows, reports, and existing documentation.

Identify and categorize:

- All application screens and modules.
- High-, medium-, and low-complexity screens.
- Approximately 30 reports, validating the actual report count where possible.
- Forms, grids, dropdowns, validations, business rules, and navigation.
- Shared components, authentication, menus, and reusable functionality.
- Backend functionality and integrations required in the .NET application.
- Opportunities to reuse existing business logic, queries, or application components.
- Technical risks and dependencies that could affect development effort.

The target is a .NET application. Estimate the effort to implement the required functionality in .NET based on the actual legacy application's complexity. Do not assume that a simple technology conversion is sufficient.

2. Build a Realistic Four-Month Development Estimate

There will be ONE developer responsible for both backend and frontend development in .NET, using Windsurf AI as a coding assistant.

The business objective is to complete the defined development scope within a maximum of four months. Assess whether this is feasible using realistic calculations.

Use the following approach:

A. Screen-level estimation

Classify every identified screen as High, Medium, or Low complexity.

For each category, calculate:

- Number of screens.
- Estimated average development effort per screen in person-days.
- Total person-days.
- Total development hours.

Consider UI implementation, backend logic, validations, business rules, data access, integration with other components, debugging, and developer-level verification.

Do not assign the same effort to every screen without justification. Explain the criteria used to classify complexity.

B. Report development estimation

The application has approximately 30 reports.

Use one report per developer working day as the initial planning assumption, then calculate:

30 reports × 1 working day per report = 30 person-days = 240 hours.

This is a baseline planning assumption, not a verified estimate for every report. Inspect report complexity and adjust the effort where required.

Account for data retrieval, filtering, grouping, calculations, formatting, parameter handling, and export functionality where applicable.

Clearly explain whether one report per day is achievable for the identified reports. Do not double-count report development effort already included in screen or module estimates.

C. Other development activities

Estimate the developer's effort for:

- Shared application foundation and reusable components.
- .NET project structure and application architecture implementation.
- Authentication and authorization, if required.
- Common layouts, navigation, and shared controls.
- Backend and frontend integration.
- Application-level business logic and data-access implementation.
- Coding-related debugging, code review, and developer-level unit/component verification.

Include these activities only where they are actually required. Avoid double-counting functionality already covered in screen or report estimates.

3. Prove Whether Four Months Is Feasible

Prepare a transparent calculation of total effort in person-days and hours.

Use:

- 1 person-day = 8 working hours.
- Approximately 17–18 working weeks in four calendar months, depending on the actual project dates.
- Realistic working days, holidays, routine meetings, and normal development overhead.
- A reasonable contingency for risks and legacy-code complexity.
- Evidence-based assumptions about productivity improvements from Windsurf AI.

Calculate the single developer's realistic available capacity over four months. Compare it with the total estimated effort.

Show the calculation clearly:

Total development effort = Screen development + Report development + Other required development activities, with overlapping work removed.

Available capacity = Realistic productive working days available to one developer during the four-month period.

Important: Do not force the estimate to fit four months by arbitrarily reducing effort. If the scope exceeds available capacity, identify the gap and recommend practical scope prioritization or phased development. Do not claim that the complete scope can be delivered within four months unless the calculations support that conclusion.

4. Strict Scope Boundaries

The estimate must cover application development only.

Explicitly exclude all effort and timelines for:

- Dedicated QA team testing.
- UAT execution and business-user validation.
- Post-UAT bug-fixing cycles and rework.
- DBA team activities and database administration.
- DevOps activities, CI/CD ownership, and deployment execution.
- Production deployment, production release, and go-live.
- Post-production support and warranty activities.

The developer's normal coding-related debugging and unit/component verification may be included as part of development.

Database-related application code or queries required by the .NET application may be included, but do not estimate separate DBA activities.

Do not present UAT, QA, production deployment, or go-live as completed within the four-month development commitment.

5. Create a Leadership-Ready PowerPoint — Maximum 3 Slides

Create no more than three slides. Use fewer slides if the information can be communicated more clearly.

Slide 1 — Executive Summary and Four-Month Development Scope

Include:

- Project name: FHN Insurance Legacy Application Modernization to .NET.
- Single-developer delivery model.
- Development-only scope.
- Verified counts of screens, modules, and reports.
- High-, medium-, and low-complexity distribution.
- Total estimated person-days and hours.
- Four-month available development capacity.
- A clear conclusion on whether the proposed scope fits within four months.

Highlight that four months refers exclusively to development, not the complete project lifecycle.

Slide 2 — Screen, Report, and Development Effort Breakdown

Present a clear table with:

- Screen complexity: High, Medium, Low.
- Number of screens in each category.
- Average effort per screen.
- Total effort in person-days and hours.
- Report development: approximately 30 reports, with the one-report-per-working-day baseline and any justified adjustments.
- Other development activities and their estimated effort.
- Contingency and relevant assumptions.

Show the total calculation and ensure there is no double-counting.

Use readable tables or a simple chart. The leadership team must be able to understand where the development effort will be spent and why.

Slide 3 — Four-Month Development Roadmap, Assumptions, and Exclusions

Show a realistic phased roadmap for one developer, using months or weeks.

Include the sequence of:

1. Legacy application analysis and development planning.
2. Shared .NET foundation and common components.
3. High-, medium-, and low-complexity screen implementation.
4. Report development and application integration, scheduled alongside other work where feasible.
5. Development-level verification and completion of the defined development scope.

The phases may overlap where technically sensible. Do not schedule the same developer at full capacity across multiple simultaneous activities.

Include key assumptions, major dependencies, and risks.

Add a clear scope exclusion statement:

"Four-month estimate covers application development only. Dedicated QA, UAT, post-UAT bug fixes, DBA activities, DevOps, production deployment, and go-live are excluded. These activities will be planned separately after development, subject to the relevant team's assessment and business approval."

Do not promise a production release date or end-to-end project completion date.
